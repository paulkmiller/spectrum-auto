About the shop

Why the shop works this way

Spectrum is a very small single-member shop — me, Sean Slattery. When you call, you're talking to the person who will be doing the work on your car, and when you pick it up, you're talking to him again.

I'm not interested in quick turnarounds, canned prefabricated solutions, or large profits. I believe in doing everything right, even if it takes longer. That's the whole business model — the value in what I charge comes out of labor and care, not markup on a box.

The shop is a garage bay in an industrial park in Papillion, alongside a row of other trades. It isn't a showroom, and it isn't trying to be. What's in it is measurement equipment, 3D printers, and the time to use them properly.

Background

Sean Slattery

Owner · Installer · Fabricator
Fifteen-ish years in mobile electronics

I grew up around this. My dad was an industrial engineer and a sound guy before he moved into finance, and a serious car guy on top of it — deep into music and home audio. He and I built guitar amplifiers together, and I was reading schematics and learning basic electrical physics well before I was old enough to drive.

Cars caught me late in high school and car audio hooked me immediately. I started as an installer at Best Buy, worked up from the bottom, and ran my own install bay there for several years — picking up bench time at Inphase and Heartland Audio along the way.

From there, a handful of years at Stereo West: three at the Elkhorn branch until it closed, then install bay manager at the main store. Between that pace and stretches away on Army Reserve duty, I moved to Heartland Audio full time for a steadier schedule — and used that stretch to build this business. Spectrum opened its doors in November.

CAD I learned because I had to. It became central to this industry a few years ago, and the bigger shops can afford to employ independent designers — I can't, so I taught myself. It has turned into the part of the job I like most.
